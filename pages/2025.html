Meaux Burreax Pre-Season Tier List

Another draft closed, which means another tier list is owed. Your favorite managers have been hard at work, sweating over every penny. Come take a look at what they’ve been cooking!

Theodore’s Meat

The crème de la crème. Smoked to perfection.

Noah

We bet you didn’t see this one coming. The perennial chart-topper has swiftly entered a new era. Goodbye A.J. Brown. Goodbye Josh Allen. Instead, Noah will be welcoming Saquon Barkley, who cleared 2,000 yards last year, and Jonathan Taylor, the 26 year-old who is just off his second 100 yards per game season. If this backfield duo can stay healthy and accomplish anywhere near their prior season marks, this team will be an automatic contender. Add to that Lamar Jackson, the greatest rushing quarterback since Michael Vick, and you have a pack of dogs ready to run their opponents into the ground. There is, of course, risk. If any of these stars misses action, there is no obvious replacement. George Pickens, D.J. Moore, and Deebo Samuel, while good value, don’t provide the game-changing upside of Noah’s running backs. Still, if there’s any roster a manager wants to build around, it’s this one. This team is a snowball careening downhill, and it’s only picking up speed from here.

Weakness: Wide Receivers. Noah’s putting a lot of faith in the abilities of George Pickens and D.J. Moore.

Tom

Once again, Tom got his guy. No, not Christian McCaffrey. Someone even more game-breaking: Alec Pierce. Jokes aside, Tom has built an elite squadron that looks to be among the most robust in the league (and he’ll be needing that after last year’s injury mishaps). Dak Prescott, coming off of a dismal, injury-curtailed season, leads a tight team of receivers: Justin Jefferson, who just received an upgrade in quarterback, and Jaxon Smith-Njigba, who is looking to build on his breakout sophomore campaign. Even more intriguing than that, however, are Tom’s running back selections. Kyren Williams is quietly coming off of his best fantasy season yet, and Josh Jacobs, a seasoned veteran at this point, saw a major revival in Green Bay last year. As if that’s not enough, Tom can enjoy (or suffer, depending on how you look at it), a litany of great flex options. Will he opt for old, reliable David Montgomery? Or will he plug in J.K. Dobbins and Chris Godwin, both of whom have shown flashes of brilliance but have struggled to stay healthy lately? Regardless of Tom’s choices, this tank of a team promises to be imposing any given week.

Weakness: Quarterback. Dak was once elite. Today, he’s not carrying a team.

Bob’s Hoagies

Sometimes you can’t beat the classics. A Fremont staple. An American staple.

Theo

Despite what we just told you about Tom, this may be the strongest overall team from top to bottom. When DeVonta Smith, Joe Mixon, Chris Olave, and Brandon Aiyuk are sitting pretty on your bench, you know you’ve got a scary group. And though it’s well-rounded, there’s plenty of upside at the very top with Christian McCaffrey and Derrick Henry, two of the greatest to ever do it. Theo’s quarterback, Baker Mayfield, had a stellar performance last season and will be looking to pick up where he left off. Meanwhile, he can rely on some fantastic second options in Mike Evans, who never seems to regress despite his age, and Breece Hall, a true blue chip back who was let down by an Aaron Rodgers offensive system last year but has a brand new coach and quarterback. So why is he in this tier? The answer is a lot of age and ifs. On one hand, for this team to shine, we need to see Breece Hall bounce back from a lackluster previous season. On the other, Baker Mayfield needs to avoid sliding back into his Browns form, and Christian McCaffrey has yet to show that he can dazzle post injury. As great as Henry and Evans were last year, they’re officially approaching old man territory--and the cliff that comes with it. So, without any 2025 data, this team is a bit of a Rorschach. An unstoppable juggernaut? Or a limp collection of has-beens? Time will tell.

Weakness: Tight end. We don’t think Evan Engram is it. But if tight end is your weakness, you’re in a pretty good spot.

Yiding

Truthfully, it would be hard for Yiding to go anywhere but up from last year, but this draft was a genuine leap. At first glance, this team’s potential might not seem immediately apparent: a unit with no real quarterback and no real star. Look again. A.J. Brown is a stud who will continue to get his. Yes, target share will always be a question on the Eagles, but that Super Bowl-winning offense remains one of the most potent in the league, and A.J. Brown remains the top dog in that receiver unit. Tony Pollard, though he struggles at the goal line, is one of the most dynamic runners in the game with the potential to erupt at any time. James Cook is a workhorse and an integral component of the Bills’ preeminent running threat. Garrett Wilson is brimming with natural talent that’s begging to be unlocked by a halfway competent offense, while Ladd McConkey just had one of the most impressive rookie campaigns of 2024. The cherry on top of this well-conceived squad is that the bench is just about ideal. Colston Loveland and Cam Skattebo are two intriguing rookie wildcards who more than proved their worth in college, and Marvin Harrison Jr. is finally in the right role for a team that’s looking to win a championship.

Weakness: Quarterback. Justin Fields will trick you into thinking he’s good. He’s not.

Vinny

While Vinny’s draft hasn’t garnered the same amount of chatter as other teams, we think this one has potential to sneak up on everyone. Let’s start with the basics. Vinny got potentially the best quarterback deal of the evening with Josh Allen, who just finished an MVP season (and married Hailee Steinfeld). This is a guy who can throw bombs, scramble out of pocket, and push his tush like the best of them. But if a quarterback alone can’t sell you, perhaps Vinny’s receivers can. Brock Bowers has already established himself as the most dominant tight end in the NFL as a rookie. Let that sink in for a minute. With a newly Pete Carroll-led Raiders offense, he just could be the next Travis Kelce. And that’s not all. Fresh-faced Puka Nacua enters his third year as a premier wide receiver (despite missing some time last year), while Terry McLaurin bloomed under the new stewardship of Jayden Daniels. Finally, DK Metcalf and Keenan Allen round out this eye-popping air attack. His running back room leaves something to be desired, but as long as Vinny can keep it off the ground, his star status will rise.

Weakness: Running backs. The inverse of last year. Vinny is packed to the gills with amazing receivers, yet he will have to choose between Rachaad White and Jerome Ford in his second starting halfback slot.

Wenlong

Though Wenlong’s bidding in this year’s draft remained as erratic as ever, it may have paid off this time. Whether or not by design, he levied some steep fees on other managers for players of questionable value, while securing a reasonable roster for himself. That roster starts with the sacred reunion of Wenlong and Joe Burrow, whom Wenlong once deemed inferior to Kirk Cousins. But the Comeback Kid led the league in passing yards last year and even used his legs to moderate effect, perhaps reigniting a fire deep within the third-year manager. Wenlong made sure to spread the talent around the roster, however. Jahmyr Gibbs emerged last season as the primary back in a star-studded Lions offense, showcasing his talent as a runner and a receiver. Though T.J. Hockenson had somewhat of a disappointing season last year, a new quarterback could snap him right back into form. And Davante Adams, now entering his age 33 season, has shown he can still hang with the young guns. It remains to be seen whether Joe Burrow can stay off Wenlong’s bench, but until then, this is no team to sleep on.

Weakness: Wide receivers. Wenlong’s top three receivers have 26 seasons between them. They just aren’t packing the same punch.

All The Shit In My Fridge Right Now

Why did you leave all this shit? Some good stuff in there if you dig around, but between the hamburger buns, the Costco potato salad, and the ten thousand seltzers, there’s not a ton of sustenance.

Peggy

There’s a vision here for sure. Amon-Ra St. Brown and Bijan Robinson are bound to be studs, while Tyreek Hill carries significant upside (along with significant baggage), but the roster just doesn’t totally work. For starters, her starter. As talented as Justin Herbert is, the big man has failed to live up to his early career benchmarks, and Jim Harbaugh (though flawless as a coach in the real world) has proven himself to be a detriment to his quarterbacks’ fantasy numbers. Dallas Goedert, who plays a bit part in a committee offense, is borderline unplayable for fantasy owners. And nothing about Xavier Worthy’s game last season suggested he’s poised to make the jump in his second year. If you squint hard enough, you might be able to fool yourself into thinking this team is a true contender, but we aren’t in the business of trading on unadulterated hope.

Weakness: Quarterback. Justin Herbert has continuously regressed over the past three seasons. Will this be the year he puts it together again?

Sam

It’s not a coincidence that the teams with two Dolphins players each end up in this part of the tier list. We’re not saying a deep playoff run isn’t possible. We’re just saying we’ll believe it when we see it. A few years ago, this team could’ve been deadly, but these days, it feels like a defanged leviathan of yore. Sam is known as a bit of a clairvoyant in this league, so perhaps Dr. Strange is envisioning paths we can’t even fathom. But leaning on Ashton Jeanty in his first NFL season, DeVon Achane in a very shaky offense, CeeDee Lamb after a down year, and washed-up hall-of-famers, Cooper Kupp, Nick Chubb, and Travis Kelce, just does not seem prudent. Maybe it’s us. Maybe there’s no such thing as magic.

Weakness: Wide receivers. 35 year-old Adam Thielen isn’t going to save you.

Lu Rou Fan

You’ve been biking for six hours and your tank is empty. This small town seems to only serve pork rice. It hits different for sure--perhaps not in the way you want it to.

David

Perhaps it’s wrong to tell the reigning Meaux Burreaux champion that his team doesn’t have the juice. Then again, that’s essentially what we’re saying. Though Chase Brown was a tremendous value last season when he usurped Zack Moss’s starting role in Cincinnati, it’s not entirely obvious to us that he’s vaulted himself into a top five running back spot. Yet, that’s where he needs to be in order to keep this team competitive. Likewise, Drake London finished ninth last year in receptions and touchdowns--more than respectable, but not the one to carry the team over the finish line, and with so many question marks around Michael Penix Jr.’s progress next season, this is not a bet we’d make. Jalen Hurts figures to notch another solid season, but there just isn’t a strong enough supporting cast around him to believe that David is going to make a legendary back-to-back run. Between Travis Hunter, Kaleb Johnson, Jauan Jennings, and Rashid Shaheed, David’s team carries loads of potential. But potential doesn’t win games.

Weakness: Running backs. Even if Chase Brown matches his breakout season last year, D’Andre Swift and Kaleb Johnson are weak second options.

Goutham

All of this sweating over pennies, and where did it bring him? Right back here. First let’s talk about the big spend. Goutham paid top dollar for Ja’Marr Chase (tied with Saquon Barkley for the most this year) and not completely without reason. Chase finished the 2024 season first in total yards gained, outpacing second-place Justin Jefferson by nearly 200 yards. But if we’re being honest, that says more about the state of wide receiver play last year than it does about Chase’s sheer dominance. That performance would place him third in the previous two years, and while his touchdown count was undoubtedly impressive, that will be a hard feat to replicate with the emergence of Chase Brown and the ever-lurking Tee Higgins. Of course Chase is a top wideout in the league. He’s just not the top wideout. But with that out of the way, let’s take a gander at the rest of the team. Spoiler alert: we have some questions. Rookie running back Omarion Hampton is essentially an unknown being plunked into a system run by a coach (Jim Harbaugh) who loves a good committee. He might be great, but it feels early to roll out the red carpet--even in Los Angeles. Zay Flowers has yet to make the leap, while Travis Etienne has thoroughly regressed. And though Bucky Irving and Bo Nix were certainly trending up at the end of last season, that was quite a long time ago, and we don’t have a huge sample of games to suggest they’re destined to continue. Look, if these are your guys, we understand feeling hyped about them, but we are pressing X to doubt.

Weakness: Quarterback. Bo Nix had a decent rookie campaign, but he’ll still need to make a leap if he wants to have a large fantasy impact.

Dubious Food

Link’s reaction says it all. It might give you an iota of energy if you can hold your nose.

Dongbo

If crypto were a team, it’d be this team. Just pure speculation--and for what? At running back, Dongbo is starting rookie TreVeyon Henderson, who’s being dropped into a Patriots offense that has yet to find its feet and who might not even be the best tailback drafted this year from his college (but hey, at least he’s not headed to jail like that other guy). That’s a pretty big bet, but it’s not even close to Dongbo’s biggest. Dongbo’s boldest move on draft night was shelling out for Malik Nabers, who went for the seventh highest price overall (tied with Jahmyr Gibbs). Nabers had an immaculate rookie season, but the Giants offense is going to look a lot different this year, and at that price, the expectation is that Nabers cracks the top three wideouts. It’s not inconceivable, but it’s far from a lock. Next up is RJ Harvey, another rookie whose situation is a complete unknown. Sure, Sean Payton loves a good run, and J.K. Dobbins is injury-prone, but Harvey could just as easily flame out or struggle to get touches. And then there’s the backup receivers. Rome Odunze and Jameson Williams have struggled to stamp their place in their respective offenses. Between the two of them, there’s a mother lode of potential, but so far, untapped. Chuba Hubbard and Tee Higgins offer a weak attempt at stability, but those two are not game winners on their own. We suppose this team could be headed for the moon, but fundamentals suggest an FTX-level collapse. The only thing sure about this team is how useless Kyle Pitts is about to be.

Weakness: Tight end. Welcome to Kyle Pitts hell.

Michael

The worst part about this team is that it appears to be a vision successfully executed. It’s like staring into an abyss--it’s impossible to know where to begin. Well, how about we begin with the pair of $110 receivers that are strangely--fittingly--sitting on his bench? Nico Collins surprised a lot of folks when he burst onto the scene two years ago, but a late season injury and a C.J. Stroud slide last year make us wonder if his best days are behind him. Regardless, for the price Michael is paying, he should expect a Houston renaissance for the fifth-year, which is a lot to ask of a player who’s never breached 90 yards per game. Brian Thomas Jr., on the other hand, surged in his rookie season, though it’s worth noting that his targets and numbers went up when (checks notes) Mac Jones was throwing to him. But okay, we hear you. Questionable as those picks may be, they don’t really sound that bad. And you’d be right. The problem is who’s behind these two brave souls: no one. Michael proceeded to spend the rest of the night buying lottery ticket after lottery ticket. Kenneth Walker III is on a one-track path to the bench, and his replacement, Zach Charbonnet (who Michael also owns), probably won’t fare better. Ricky Pearsall might be willing to take a bullet for his teammates, but he’s so far shown nothing to suggest he can be a reliable on-field contributor. Emeka Egbuka and Matthew Golden are rookies desperately clawing their way into WR3 situations. And Tyrone Tracy Jr. was a so-so back for a middling team that’s going to have to wrestle for the job with rookie Cam Skattebo. This is not a team. This is a collection of atoms randomly bouncing around the atmosphere, drafted by an idiot, full of sound and fury signifying nothing.

Weakness: An inability to not draft his guys. I’ve never seen a man so lost in the sauce.
