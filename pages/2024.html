2024 Pre-Season Rankings

All years

Straight to the Moon

Tom

Tom got his guy. With the second pick of the night Tom was down half of his budget, but he was up one Christian McCaffrey. And, as long as the superstar running back can remain healthy, he will be worth the hefty price. McCaffrey is yards ahead of the next closest offensive talent in terms of fantasy (and real) production in 2024. But Tom didn’t kick his feet up and put his hands over his head. Instead, he waited. And deep into the recesses of this draft, he found the bargains. Travis Kelce was not too long ago the most dominant tight end in football, and it wasn’t even close. He may now be primarily known as Mr. Pfizer or future Mr. Swift, but he is still a beast to be reckoned with--and very much in the running for top tight end of 2024. Tom scooped up Kelce along with David Montgomery and Florida-based star receivers, Mike Evans and Jaylen Waddle. But perhaps the best snag came at the very end when Tom selected Chuba Hubbard as a late round flier. After that blockbuster McCaffrey pick, Tom put on a masterclass of drafting on a shoestring budget, and the result is a roster built to demolish anyone in its path to a championship.

Noah

Where Tom is a front loaded Death Star pointed straight at every Mo Burreaux roster, Noah brings balance to the fantasy forces. Don’t let this team’s depth fool you, it’s got plenty of bite at the top. Josh Allen is the greatest regular season quarterback in the league, and he shows no signs of stopping. AJ Brown will remain the deadliest offensive weapon on one of the most offensively stacked teams. Stefon Diggs should welcome the change of scenery in Houston and has been flying under the radar after a slight dip in production in 2023. And Calvin Ridley quietly burst onto the scene late last season. But we know what you’re thinking. What about the running backs? It’s true, Noah failed to splash the cash on a critical position in fantasy football, but where he did spend, he got excellent value. Tony Pollard was erroneously anointed at the beginning of last season after winning sole carrying responsibilities. As it turns out, his effectiveness has always been as a part of a dual run package, and he looks to return to form in Tennessee. On the flip side, D’Andre Swift had a statistically fantastic season with Philadelphia but was robbed of most of the touchdowns he helped earn. Now, with the tush push shackles off in Chicago, Swift is primed to eat. But the best part is, there’s no single point of failure on this team. Where one falters, another is waiting to be plugged in.

Absolutely Electric

Goutham

This team is good. Really good. Cooper Kupp and Saquon Barkley’s savvy veteran presence mix nicely with high upside rookies like Ladd McConkey and Brian Thomas Jr. But it’s the young experienced corps of Jalen Hurts, Travis Etienne Jr., and Nico Collins that raise this team to another level. Though Kupp may have lost a step due to injury and the ascendency of Puka Nacua, we’d be surprised if he doesn’t have something to say about it in 2024. And don’t discount Khalil Shakir, who will have minimal competition for Josh Allen’s targets. If there’s one weakness in this roster, it’s at the tight end position. Pat Freiermuth doesn’t fly off the stat sheet, and there’s nothing to indicate a real change of fortunes in Pittsburgh. That’s something that Goutham can live with, and with Nick Chubb waiting in the wings, this team may only get better.

Peggy

With studs like Bijan Robinson, Kenneth Walker III, and JK Dobbins, Peggy will be ready this fall to pound the ground and attack those holes. But it doesn’t have to end there. If the run game isn’t her style, she can count on steady production from star receiver Amon-Ra St. Brown and David Njoku in the tight end slot. Furthermore, Jordan Addison will be looking to build on his promising (if frustratingly inconsistent) rookie campaign. As long as Peggy’s not asked to rely too heavily on her relatively weak bench this season, she might just find herself in another championship game. Minus some vibes points for Harrison Butker and the Cowboys defense.

Vinny

Vinny’s team is Bourne again. As we’ve learned from prior seasons, Vinny loves his ascendant running backs, and the Breece Hall, Kyren Williams combo fits the bill perfectly. If these two can match their production from last season, Vinny will be in good shape. If they can improve, Vinny will be lethal. Furthermore, Dak Prescott is coming off a terrific rebound season in which he finished third among quarterbacks in fantasy points, and Mark Andrews is poised to be one of the best tight ends after an injury cut his season short last year. We do have some questions about Vinny’s receivers, as DJ Moore, George Pickens, Jakobi Meyers, and an aging DeAndre Hopkins wouldn’t normally hold the mantle of a top flight team, but Vinny’s strengths make this roster impossible to disregard.

Best of the Consolation Bracket

David

By all accounts this is an acceptable--even good--squad. But from our vantage, it just doesn’t totally jell. As much as we love George Kittle, he’s a bit pricey to have as a backup tight end. And though we can’t complain with the price tag on Joe Burrow, David will similarly get little use out of him. Still, David can enjoy a strong ground game with the powerful one-two punch of Jonathan Taylor and Rachaad White. Add on the YAC king, Deebo Samuel, line up Ezekiel Elliott at center, and this team might just run circles around everybody.

Dongbo

Leaving the draft halfway through proved to be an incredibly injudicious decision when Dongbo picked up his third quarterback for a considerable amount of money. Though, honestly, he might just get away with it. Sure, he has no playable tight end, but that’s nothing a good trade can’t fix--plus, we know a guy with George Kittle sitting around on the bench. And yeah, maybe his bench is thinner than the hair on Tyler Lockett’s head. And okay, fine. Perhaps the Saints defense wasn’t the most inspiring pick. But listen: can you really go wrong with a starting lineup of Patrick Mahomes, Ja’Marr Chase, Derrick Henry, and James Cook? Well, Dongbo sure as hell seems to be trying.

Theo

It’s a bit hard to discern a clear image from the general malaise that is Theo’s team. No one truly stands out--and between Kyler Murray, DeVonta Smith, Garrett Wilson, Raheem Mostert, Josh Jacobs, Aaron Jones, and Younghoe Koo, no one really stands tall either. But okay, if we were to compliment this team, we might start with Michael Pittman Jr., who has seemingly gotten better with each passing year. And let’s just say we admire the tenacity of picking Garrett Wilson for another round of fantasy football after a lot of hand-wringing about him in Theo’s inaugural season. Aaron Jones may have declined an appreciable amount last year, but the veteran is still of potentially tremendous value as the lead back to Sam Darnold. Is this team a winner? Hard to say. Will it make a splash when Theo inevitably blows it up for a platoon of Texans midway through the season? Almost definitely.

Sam

One wouldn’t think that such an artful and knowledgeable football head like Sam would demonstrate such sheer disdain for the draft, but time and time again, this seems to be his modus operandi. Like his tinfoil hat commander in chief, Aaron Rodgers, Sam appears hellbent on proving he can win with any old scrub. We just wonder: can he? Or are his glory years but a walking shadow? There are things to like. Sam LaPorta could easily be the best tight end in the league. Puka Nacua is coming off an astounding rookie season. And Justin Jefferson on an aspiration-less team is still Justin Jefferson. But these guys were not cheap, and there’s no guarantee they can stay on their trajectories. Meanwhile, Sam is running it back with Zach Charbonnet and Adam Thielen. Really? Will Sam’s team be a contender? Probably. Will it be because of his draft? No.

CeeDeez Nuts

Michael

CeeDee Lamb should feel right at home on this team. And by that we mean that it’s the type of giga-brain squad Jerry Jones might assemble. This bench is full of flashy young receivers: Rashee Rice (who should be suspended), Jameson Williams (who has yet to put a successful season together), Rome Odunze (who has to fight his way out of a crowded wide-out room with a rookie quarterback), Jaxon Smith-Njigba, and Malik Nabers (who has, checks notes, Daniel Jones throwing to him). And his running back room fares no better. Najee Harris’ fantasy value has slid in recent years, while James Conner is only getting older. Michael will pin his hopes on De’Von Achane once again catching Miami lightning in a bottle, but with the crowded backfield, that is far from a guarantee. Jesus Christ--as much as it doesn’t really matter, Michael’s kicker, Brett Maher, doesn’t even have a real life team, and somehow that just seems symbolic. Like the late great Roger Ebert didn’t say about a Francis Ford Coppola film: this roster is a triumph of style over substance.

IrreDeemable Trash

Yiding

Yiding better hope that the consonantly-named rookie son of Hall-of-Famer Marvin Harrison is worth the pretty penny he paid because there otherwise isn’t a whole lot to love here. As much as the corners covering Tyreek Hill might feel like they’re on an island, John Donne taught us that no man is an island, and Hill won’t be winning these games by himself. For the sake of argument, let’s say that Marvin Harrison Jr. has an all-pro first season, Evan Engram remains the second best tight end, Isiah Pacheco’s star status continues to rise, and Javonte Williams bounces back from his middling post-injury season. We still have significant doubts that Anthony Richardson and the rest of the squad can carry Yiding across the finish line. A strong bench might be able to rectify some of the misses on these starters, but Joshua Palmer, Curtis Samuel, and Rico Dowdle are neither good nor inspiring enough to resuscitate this dead-in-the-water roster.

Wenlong

This was a disastrous first draft for the second-year manager. It’s hard to pick out any single standout in this crowd and that’s not due to a bounty of them. Jahmyr Gibbs and Gus Edwards are solid starting running back choices, but not as the primary scorers of an offense. And as much as this team lacks depth (with ponderous picks like Austin Ekeler, Zamir White, Taysom Hill, and Christian Watson), it doesn’t have the top-heavy gravitas to justify it. Davante Adams will deal with Gardner Minshew throwing to him, Brandon Aiyuk is a mere cog in the 49ers offense, and Chris Olave is still very much unproven at the top tier. And it’s not for nought that nine of Wenlong’s fifteen players represent four of the currently crummiest teams in football: the Packers, the Saints, the Raiders, and the Commanders. With a winning culture like that, how can he not succeed? Strikingly, Wenlong’s most solid picks were probably his kicker, Justin Tucker, and the Browns defense. Leaning on your kicker and your defense in fantasy is not a winning formula and neither is this roster.
